# Assassin

Move one of your pieces and capture another of your own pieces with it.

# Disintegration

Remove one of your own Pawns from the chessboard, and set it aside. It is now *dead*, and cannot be brought back into play with another card.

# Doomsayer

The next player who pronounces the name of a piece, except "King," loses one piece of that type. If he doesn't own a piece of that type, this card remains in effect. When you play this card, your opponent has the option to name a piece immediately.

# Fanatic

Move one of your Pawns forward three squares, provided the path is clear. It may not capture on this move, or be captured *en passant*.

# Annexation

Move one or two of your Pawns forward, two squares each. Neither one may make a capture. A Pawn which was on its starting square may still be captured *en passant* after this move, if an enemy Pawn is in position to do so.

# Forced March

Move one or two of your Pawns sideways, in either direction, one square each.

# Guardian

Move one of your Pawns forward (you may move two squares if it the Pawn is second rank). Your piece which was just behind the Pawn may follow, so it remains directly behind. Your Pawn is thus protected from capture *en passant*. Play this card on your move, instead of making a regular move.

# Heresy

Each Bishop that can do so must be moved to an adjacent empty square by its owner, thus changing the color of the squares it moves on. Your opponent must move his Bishops first.

# Holy War

Swap the positions of one of your Knights and one of your Bishops.

# Madman

For this move, one of your pawns can move like a King in a game of checkers, by jumping diagonally over pieces from either side. It may make as many jumps as it can, in any direction. However, the pieces it jumps are not captured.

# Pacifism

One of your pieces, except your King, becomes non-violent for the rest of the game. Place a marker on it. It can no longer capture another piece, nor may it be captured.

# Squaring the Circle

You may play this Card Only when three of the four corners of the chessboard are occupied. Move any one of your pieces to the empty corner.

# Vendetta

Each player must now use his move to capture one of his opponent's pieces, if he can do so. This effect lasts until one of the players *cannot* make a capture. You are not required to play a card to make a capture, even if you have a card that would let you capture.

# Bog

Play this card after your opponent moves a Rook, Bishop or Queen two squares or more. His piece cannot make the planned move; it stops after moving one square in the chosen direction.

# Cowardice

Move one of your opponent's Pawns one or two squares backward. It may not enter or cross an occupied square.

# Doppelganger

Move one of your pieces (except a Pawn) as if it were a piece of the same kind as the one your opponent has just moved. You cannot capture a piece with this move.

# Dubbing

For this turn, one of your pieces may move as if it were a Knight. You cannot capture a piece with this move.

# Earthquake

Turn the chessboard 90 degrees in any direction. Pawns move away from their owner, any in the new last rank is promoted, your opponent promoting first. Pawns in the first rank can move one or two squares forward. If undone, the rotation is reversed with the same rules.

# Figure Dance

Each piece in any corner of the chessboard moves counterclockwise to the next corner. These moves happen at the same time; no pieces are captured, Pawns which reach the last rank are promoted.

# No Quarter

Play this card after you capture any enemy piece without using a card. The captured piece is now *dead* and cannot be brought back into play with another card.

# Rebirth

Move one enemy piece to any square it could have occupied at the beginning of the game. The square must be empty or contain one of your pieces. If one of your pieces is in the square, it is captured.

# Revenge

Remove one of your opponent's Pawns from the chessboard. It is regarded as captured.

# Toll

Play this card when your opponent has just moved a piece toward you across the "frontier" - the line separating your half of the board from his. He must "pay" by choosing a Pawn to lose. If he does not wish to pay, his turn is canceled. He gets back any card he used, but he cannot make another move.

# Anathema

Swap the positions of a Bishop and a Rook belonging to your opponent.

# Crab

One of your Pawns becomes a Crab for the rest of the game. Place a marker underneath it as a reminder. A Crab moves and captures diagonally, like a Bishop, but only forward and only one square at a time. If a Crab reaches the last rank, it is promoted like a regular Pawn.

# Dark Mirror

On this move, one of your Pawns can capture by moving diagonally backward instead of forward.

# Forbidden City

Place a marker in any unoccupied square. No pieces can enter this square, or pass through it, for the rest of the game. Knights and other "jumping" pieces may still pass over it.

# Confabulation

Make a legal move which puts two of your pieces (other than Kings) on the same square. These two pieces "merge" into a new piece. It can move, capture, and be affected by cards like either one of them. Move the two pieces together. Confabulated Pawns cannot promote.

# Ghostwalk

On this move, one of your pieces gains the ability to pass through your own pieces. It may not pass through enemy pieces, and must end its move on an empty square, without capturing. Its move must be otherwise legal.

# Irresistible Force

Move one of your Pawns to the occupied square in front of it, by pushing the piece occupying that square to the next square. If that square is occupied, the piece there is pushed to the next square, and so on. If a piece is pushed from the last rank off the chessboard, it is taken! A King cannot be pushed.

# Masquerade

Move one of your pieces, except a Pawn, as if it were a Queen. You cannot capture a piece with this move.

# Panic

Your opponent has 15 seconds to make his next move. If he has not moved within this time, he loses his turn.

# Peace Talks

Remove any one "continuing effect" card from play, placing it in the owner's discard pile. The effect of that card is immediately cancelled. If any piece is left in an illegal situation, its owner must correct the problem on his next move or lose that piece.

# Truce

Capture is now forbidden. This effect lasts until a King is in check or a stalemate occurs. If a stalemate occurs, discard this card and start counting moves again for the purpose of determining stalemate.

# Vulture

Take the last card played by your opponent and put it in your hand. If each player has his own deck of cards, you must also discard your top undrawn card.

# Blessing

On this move, any one of your pieces may move any distance diagonally, as though it were a Bishop. You cannot capture with this move.

# Breakthrough

On this move, one of your Pawns can capture by moving forward. It cannot move forward unless it captures. If for any reason this Pawn could already capture by a forward move, this card lets it capture with a diagonal move instead.

# Challenge

Choose a piece of your opponent (except King or Queen). On his next move, your opponent must use that piece, or lose his turn. You may not name a piece vour opponent does not possess or cannot legally move.

# Charge!

One of your Knights can make two moves in a row, as long as it does not capture anything with its first move.

# Coup

Your King becomes a Prince for the remainder of the game. A Prince moves like a King but can be captured. Choose one of your pieces, except a Rook or a Queen, and mark it. This piece keeps its standard move, but is the new King, and your opponent will win if he checkmates it.

# Curse

Choose one of your opponent's Queens, Bishops or Rooks, and place a marker on it. This piece can only move one or two squares at a time for the rest of the game.

# Evangelists

Swap the positions of one of your Bishops and one of your opponent's Bishops.

# Haunting Memories

This card has the same effect as the last card played. It cannot be used to duplicate a unique card from your deck. It can duplicate a unique card of your opponent's!

# Man-Trap

Secretly write down the coordinates of a square which currently contains one of your pieces. The next *opposing* piece to end its move in this square is captured. Any capture it made with that move is still valid. If the enemy King springs the trap, he is unaffected.

# Onslaught

Any number of your Pawns which can legally move may all move one square forward. None of them may make a capture.

# Passing in the Night

Swap the positions of one of your Pawns and one of your opponent's Pawns, or swap two of your Pawns with two of your opponent's Pawns.

# Tournament

Swap the positions of one of your Knights and one of your opponent's Knights.

# Winged Victory

Take a Pawn which your opponent has captured, and return it to play by placing it on any of the four squares in the middle of the chessboard. The target square must be empty!

# Betrayal

One of your opponent's Pawns on your half of the board switches allegiance. Replace it with one of your own captured Pawns. His Pawn is considered dead. It cannot return to play.

# Cathedral

Swap the positions of one of your Rooks and one of your Bishops.

# Dungeon

Choose one of your opponent's pieces, except a King, and move it to any unoccupied corner of the chessboard. Your opponent cannot move this piece on his following turn.

# Fortification

Create a wall between two directly or diagonally neighboring squares anywhere on the chessboard. No pieces can cross the wall unless they have a Knight-style jumping move, or unless you use a card which "jumps" them.

# Holy Quest

Swap the positions of a Bishop and a Knight belonging to your opponent.

# Long Jump

Move one of your Knights to any square whose color is different from the one it currently occupies. You cannot capture a piece with this move.

# Lost Castle

Swap the positions of one of your Rooks and one of your opponent's Rooks.

# Sanctuary

No matter what has happened before this card is played, if your King and one of your Rooks are in the same rank or file with no pieces between them, you can "castle". Move the Rook up to your King and then have your King jump over the Rook, to the first square on the other side.

# Siege

Swap the positions of one of your Knights and one of your Rooks.

# Split Knight

Play this card when one of your Knights has the choice of taking two or more of your opponent's pieces. Capture as many of these pieces as you want (at least two). Your Knight is removed from play and regarded as captured.

# Under Elf Hill

Remove your King from the chessboard. At the beginning of your next turn, he reappears on any vacant square on any edge of the board. He may not appear in check, and may not move that turn, but otherwise, you may take a normal turn after replacing the King.

# Abduction

Your opponent must look away from the board for ten seconds, as you remove any one of his pieces except the King. He then has ten seconds to look at the board and state what piece you removed and which square it occupied. If he remembers correctly, the piece is put back in its place, If not, it is captured.

# Bombard

On this move, one of your Rooks can move in its normal straight line, jump over any one piece or one obstruction on the board, and continue in a straight line. The piece or obstruction is not affected by being jumped. At the end of its move, the Rook may make a normal capture.

# Fatal Attraction

Choose one of your pieces to become a *magnet*. Until it moves or is captured, no piece of any color (except Kings) in the eight adjacent squares can move. A piece may pass through these squares without effect, but one that stops there (whether or not it makes a capture) becomes trapped.

# Plots Within Plots

You may play two more cards that are legal to play in this moment immediately, ignoring the normal prohibition against playing more than one card per move! You may not make two *moves* unless each of the cards says it replaces a move.

# Resurrection

Return one of your captured pieces, except a King or Queen, to the board. You must place it on a vacant square it could have occupied at the beginning of the game.

# Treason

Swap the positions of a Rook and a Knight belonging to your opponent.

# Crusade

Play this card when you move a Bishop without capturing a piece. This Bishop immediately moves one more time.

# Evil Eye

Remove one of your opponent's pieces which is threatened by one of yours. It is considered captured. However, your "capturing" piece does not move, it remains where it is.

# Hostage

Play this card when your opponent captures one of your pieces. Choose one of your Pawns to be captured instead. Return your original captured piece to the board in the Pawn's square.

# Man of Straw

Play this card when your King is in check (even check-mate). It swaps positions with any one of your Pawns, as long as the new position does not place it in check.

# Merciless

Play this card when you move a Rook without capturing a piece. The Rook immediately moves one more time.

# Mystic shield

The piece you just moved cannot be captured by your opponent on his next turn. If you moved more than one piece, you must designate only one to be protected.

# Neutrality

One of your opponent's pieces, except a King or a Queen, becomes *neutral*. It can be moved by either player on his turn, can check either King, and can capture pieces of any color any time it is moved. Any card which affects either a friendly or enemy piece can affect a neutral.

# Chaos

Your opponent's move is canceled. He must make a different move, with the same piece or with another one. If he used a card, he may take it back.

# Fog of War

This card cancels the effect of any other card. Both cards are discarded. If the opposing card constituted your opponent's whole move, he may make another move, but he may not play another card.

# Hidden Passage

Move your King to any unoccupied square of the chessboard.

# Fireball

Play this card after any move which does not capture an opposing piece. The piece you moved *explodes* and is removed from play, along with all the pieces in the eight adjacent squares, whatever their color. The pieces are regarded as captured. Kings are not affected.

# Knightmare!

Your opponent's move is canceled, He must make a different move, with the same piece or with another one. If he used a card, he may take it back.

# Legacy

Choose one card from your discard pile, or from the common discard pile in a common-deck game, and return it to your hand.

# Riposte

Play this card when your opponent captures one of your pieces without using a card. Your piece stays in its place and your opponent's piece is captured instead. You lose your next move. You cannot take a King or Queen with this card.

# Think Again!

Your opponent's move is canceled. He must make a different move, with the same piece or with another one. If he used a card, he may take it back.
