## F1 — Fog refunds an uncanceled Vulture cost inside Plots (P2)

White moves Ng1–f3 and plays Curse on Black's a8 Rook. Black responds with Plots targeting Black, then Vulture, then Fog canceling White's original Curse. Both response cards were eligible when Plots opened; all actions are accepted.

Before Fog, Vulture has correctly discarded `black-deck-1-dubbing` as its extra cost and drawn `black-deck-2-assassin`. After Fog, that exact Dubbing has incorrectly moved from discard into Black's hand, and `black-deck-3-long-jump`, which should be Fog's replacement, remains in the deck. Physical inventory is conserved, but the player recovers a spent resource and the draw order changes. The earlier Vulture was not canceled. The reverse Fog→Vulture order and ending the turn directly after Vulture retain their appropriate costs.

The controlling local contract is `rules.md:548–552`, which preserves independent earlier actions/cards, and §17.3, which preserves the original reaction window. The publisher FAQ supports Vulture/proxy and Plots/Fog constituent rules (pp. 50–52), but does not explicitly resolve this exact composition. This finding concerns the unambiguous uncanceled cost and draw sequence under the current local contract; it makes no separate claim about ownership of the already-transferred physical Curse.

Likely cause: `src/game/reducer.ts:6247` reconstructs the countering player's independent response plays using only `spendCard`, which omits Vulture's additional cost and other independent effects. Vulture applies that cost separately around lines 5906–5909.

## F4 — Move-cancellation cards interrupt a required rescue and can leave no legal continuation (P1)

## F5 — Canceling the only legal move leaves stalemate unadjudicated (P1)

Chaos, Knightmare!, and Think Again! correctly cancel and prohibit repetition of the player's sole legal move, but leave that player safe with no legal continuation and `outcome: null`. Start from FEN `3k1K2/4p3/8/8/8/6r1/8/8 w - - 0 1`, White hand empty, Black hand containing one cancellation card, both decks empty. White's only legal move is Kf8–f7. White plays it; Black plays Chaos (or Knightmare!/Think Again!). The King returns to f8, f8–f7 is prohibited, White is active before its move and is not checked. Adding a White Rook on a1 supplies an alternative move and correctly allows continued play after cancellation.

## Qualified observation Q2: Neutral threat suppression during a rescue

Reachable seed 9102094 has a pending Black move in which both Kings are checked. Black neutralizes the checking White Pawn/Knight composite on g5. The engine then considers Black's King safe and permits completion because the prospective White controller is independently in check. `legallyAttacksRoyal` evaluates the hypothetical neutral capture's controller safety.

Neither the examined publisher passages nor local rules explicitly settles extending that restriction to ordinary pins or an independent check against the controller. Separate source review classifies this as an unresolved rule-contract choice, not a confirmed bug. The 15 advertised destinations in the saved position all have completed public-action witnesses under the engine's current interpretation, ten via Neutrality; this verifies internal consistency, not the unsettled external rule interpretation.

## Qualified observation Q1: Truce and Man-Trap consumption

A later Truce prevents a castling Rook from being captured on an opposing Man-Trap, but the engine still discards the trap. Both-color queenside fixtures retain the active Truce after the complete move, so its expiry does not explain those two outcomes. Kingside counterparts also consume the trap but subsequently end Truce through check and are weaker evidence for this particular question.

Retention is a supported interpretation, not an explicit published pairing. This remains a qualified rule-contract question and is **not counted as a confirmed bug**.
