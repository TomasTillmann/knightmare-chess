# Confabulation ordinary-play oracle scope

Source-only review; no production or test source inspected, and no engine probes executed. Sources: frozen `rules.md` §§13.1–13.3 and 15.4, frozen `cards.md` Confabulation, and the preserved official FAQ text at `/Users/tomastillmann/Random/knightmare-chess/audit/ten-hour-2026-09-08/official-faq.txt`.

- **Publisher explicit:** the printed Confabulation text allows the combined piece to move and capture as either component, and says the components move together. Thus a union of component move/capture geometry is appropriate, with ordinary occupancy and royal safety still applying. Threat generation follows from the capture powers; it must use Pawn capture geometry rather than Pawn quiet movement.
- **Publisher explicit:** Confabulated Pawns cannot promote. This does not remove the other component's movement or capture powers. Reaching the last rank without promotion is a reasonable consequence of preserving those powers, but the reviewed printed wording supplies the prohibition on promotion, not an explicit last-rank-arrival example. Label that consequence as an interpretation, rather than claiming a publisher example that is absent.
- **Publisher and local explicit:** §13.1 and FAQ page 7 (text lines 221–227) allow first- or second-rank Pawn double steps even after earlier movement, with ordinary path clearance and en-passant vulnerability. Through the either-component rule, an ordinary Pawn component supplies that power to the composite. Pawn direction remains the owner's direction in the unrotated proposed scope.
- **Local explicit:** §15.4 allows either en-passant capture or an independently legal quiet move to the same empty destination. The API default chooses capture; `enPassant: false` chooses quiet movement; `true` requires a currently available capture. Those API conventions are a local contract, not publisher API semantics.
- **Publisher explicit single-piece status; local recommended capture details:** FAQ pages 18–19 (text lines 771–784) calls a two-Pawn composite one piece for Annexation and Onslaught. §15.4 nevertheless labels general occupancy/capture handling, whole-object removal, and both original components becoming captured together as recommended rulings. That full capture-zone contract is therefore local, while single-piece status has publisher support. Keeping one physical component as carrier and the other away is an implementation representation, not an independently specified publisher rule. Verify geometry and physical conservation separately from any representation assertion.

Relevant publisher citations: FAQ page 7, lines 260–262 explicitly prohibits composite Pawn promotion; pages 17–18, lines 736–739 illustrates a Queen–Knight choosing Knight movement; page 18, lines 744–767 explains component effects and restoration; pages 18–19, lines 771–784 confirms one-piece status. None of these inspected passages explicitly resolves a Pawn-component move to the last rank without promotion. Ordinary-piece movement there follows the printed either-component power, and the no-promotion exception applies throughout; make this inference visible in oracle failure triage.

Proposed scope: one composite of two non-King roles, ordinary Kings, ordinary opponents, no castling, neutrality, Coup, other cards, transformations, or unmerging. In that scope, ordinary royal safety and the union of capture powers give a defensible independent movement oracle. Do not claim coverage of Confabulation's marker cancellation, restored components, neutral control, or Coup interactions from this oracle.
