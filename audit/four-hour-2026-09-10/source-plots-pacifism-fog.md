# Plots / Pacifism / Fog: source review

Scope: source-only review of White Ng1-f3/end; Black Ng8-f6; White Chaos; Black Plots, Pacifism on a8, then Fog targeting Chaos. No engine or test sources inspected; engine acceptance/lost Pacifism is a parent-supplied observation.

Verdict: this exact example is insufficient to confirm a cancellation bug from the sources alone. `rules.md:548` cancels only the targeted card and dependent consequences while preserving independent earlier actions **unless illegal**. `rules.md:552` expressly preserves the countering player's independent earlier cards. Pacifism was played before Fog and belongs to that countering player; restoring the separate Knight's g8-f6 move does not change ownership, identity, or target eligibility of the a8 Rook. However, target independence is not enough to establish timing independence.

Initial eligibility: `rules.md:566` records eligibility when Plots resolves, and preserves the original opposing-card response window. Chaos has returned Black to the before-move opportunity (`rules.md:536`), so a before-move Pacifism can be eligible together with Fog responding to Chaos. Pacifism does not manufacture Fog eligibility. The cards must already be in hand (`rules.md:566`); the scenario specifies that they are. `rules.md:199` and `cards.md:251` do not waive timing restrictions. The permitted Pacifism excerpts (`cards.md:41–43`, `rules.md:500–506`) state its target and protection but do not themselves state the printed before-move timing; that timing is a premise to verify against artwork or another authoritative timing source.

Dependency caveat: assuming Pacifism is before-move only, its legal timing here exists because Chaos reopened Black's move. Removing Chaos restores Black's completed Knight move. The rule excluding dependent or newly illegal consequences could therefore justify backing up Pacifism along with that canceled timing permission. Conversely, retaining a legally resolved historical play would preserve it. The bounded local text does not explicitly decide this time-window dependency; it does not authorize concluding that every uncanceled play survives regardless of how it became legal. Whether Plots and its own expenditure/allowance are dependent must be considered consistently too.

Explicit publisher material (official FAQ text line references): lines 2231–2244, page 51, permit combinations of cards legal when Plots is played and prohibit manufacturing after-move eligibility with a replacement move. Lines 2264–2280, page 52, allow Fog to select any card in an opposing trio and commit the same third card if its second card is canceled. Lines 2284–2289 expressly permit Plots plus two Fogs. These support saved response windows and selective cancellation, but none is the exact own-turn combination of before-move Pacifism with Fog canceling the move cancellation that enabled that timing. Lines 1590–1603 distinguish card cancellation from move cancellation and place Chaos after the opponent's entire turn; they do not settle the dependency either.

The printed Pacifism description (`cards.md:43`) promises its effect for the remainder of the game, subject to the ordinary cancellation/dependency rules. The publisher's Confabulation example (FAQ lines 2014–2026, page 47) explicitly distinguishes an effect whose target ceases to exist from one whose original target survives; that is evidence for physical-target dependency, not a ruling on reversed timing permissions.

Recommendation: qualify this candidate pending an explicit local policy on cancellation-created timing, or find a reproduction where Pacifism was legal without the canceled card. Do not label the exact sequence an independently publisher-confirmed bug. No engine probes were performed by this source reviewer.
