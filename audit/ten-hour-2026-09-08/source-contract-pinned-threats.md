# Pinned pieces and threat: source-only review

The local contract explicitly disqualifies an Evil Eye attacker or Split Knight actor when its proposed ordinary capture would expose its own King. That actor-eligibility rule must be distinguished from ordinary chess King-safety attack detection, where a pinned piece still attacks squares. No explicit publisher FAQ occurrence of `pinned` or `pinning` was found in the bounded whole-document keyword search.

Evidence: `rules.md` §22.3 expressly says a pinned Knight cannot claim a capture that leaves its King in check; §22.10 likewise excludes a pinned Evil Eye attacker that would expose its King. The broad `Threaten` definition and §11.7 describe threat using legal capture capability, but their express examples concern card restrictions, not an express change to ordinary chess's pinned-piece attack convention. Standard chess distinguishes a square being attacked from whether the attacker could legally move there without exposing its own King. That ordinary convention is background knowledge here, not a separately inspected external citation.

Publisher FAQ page 26 explicitly rejects Evil Eye from Pacifist or Fatal Attraction-immobilized actors because they do not threaten capture. However, its next ruling explicitly allows a King to use Evil Eye on an adjacent protected enemy Knight: the rationale permits a temporary check provided the King is safe at turn end. Thus the publisher expressly rejects a universally strict pre-card, immediately King-safe ordinary-capture requirement for Evil Eye. Applying the same rationale to a pinned actor is plausible, particularly because Evil Eye leaves that actor stationary, but **no explicit pinned-actor ruling was found**. This is a tension with the local actor-eligibility language, not proof that every pinned actor qualifies.

Publisher FAQ page 60 likewise excludes a Pacifist Knight as a Split Knight actor. It does not resolve pinning. Split Knight removes the actor as well as the victims; consequently, even a broader threat interpretation must separately reject a completed card result that exposes the acting King. A case that also removes the pinning piece could differ. The explicit local requirement remains stricter at the individual pre-card capture stage.

Royal-Pawn en-passant composition remains unresolved for a pinned capturing Pawn. The existing `source-contract-royal-pawn-ep.md` correctly labels royal en-passant safety as derived and explicitly leaves pinned capturers unresolved. Neither the broad local wording nor the absence of a FAQ pinning ruling establishes whether this unusual royal-victim removal must use ordinary attacked-square convention or fully legal en-passant capture eligibility. En passant removes the victim from a square other than the capturer's destination, so the ordinary attacked-square rule cannot alone settle this composition. A pinned Pawn may fail the local Evil Eye actor test while still attacking its ordinary diagonal squares for conventional King safety; treating those as identical concepts would erase the distinction being audited.

Classification: (1) ordinary pinned-piece attacks are the baseline chess convention with no explicit inspected Knightmare override; (2) local Evil Eye/Split Knight actor rules expressly apply legal-capture pin restrictions; (3) FAQ page 26 makes the local Evil Eye restriction suspect by direct royal-attacker counterexample, while extension to pinned nonroyal actors is derived; (4) royal-Pawn en-passant through a pinned capturer remains an unresolved composed-rule question and should not be reported as an unqualified publisher-conformance defect.
