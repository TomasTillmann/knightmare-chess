# Under Elf Hill and a hidden constituent's Crab marker

Conclusion: yes, the repository's explicit rules require the pre-merger Crab marker on the Pawn constituent to survive Under Elf Hill's temporary absence and return, regardless of which component represents the composite on the board. The published Confabulation ruling supports that marker's continued attachment to its original constituent. No reviewed source supplies an exception that destroys this marker merely because the composite temporarily leaves the board.

- `rules.md:724`, §22.4, explicitly retains the royal piece's physical identity and attached effects and distinguishes temporary absence from capture or death. `rules.md:728` makes preservation through absence and return explicit, subject only to an effect's own expiry rule.
- `cards.md:111`, Confabulation, makes the joined object act as either component and requires both physical pieces to travel together. `rules.md:453`, §15.4, preserves this combined behavior. The local recommendations concerning capture and later return do not apply to Under Elf Hill's expressly non-capture absence.
- `official-faq.pdf`, printed pp.18 and49, distinguishes pre-merger transformations from other Continuing Effects: a named transformation such as Crab continues to affect its original portion of the combined object. If Confabulation is canceled, pre-existing effects resume their pre-merger application. This expressly supports a constituent-bound Crab marker, not dependence on an implementation's carrier selection.
- `cards.md:99`, Crab, states a game-long transformation and allows promotion. The matching artwork OCR (`KC7_card1.png`) additionally preserves the explicit duration: “Applies: Until the Crab is captured or promoted.” The parent's capture/promotion expiry statement is therefore correct, although that duration line is omitted from `cards.md`. There is no temporary-absence expiry. Capture/return transformation rules in `rules.md:224` distinguish immediate rescue from a later return, and §22.4 says this absence is not capture. Confabulated Pawns cannot promote (`cards.md:111`, `rules.md:453`), and §22.4's royal return itself does not promote.
- `rules.md:215` requires a Continuing Effect whose initial condition stops being true to remain suspended rather than be discarded. Even if royal status changes how Crab applies, that does not establish destruction of its retained marker.

Movement is a separate question. `source-review-coup-crab-movement.md` identifies a supported analogy, not an express Crab-specific ruling: the published PALADIN→COUP ruling on FAQ p.20 lets later Coup override the transformation's movement. Neither that ruling nor the reviewed Confabulation and Coup material on pp.19–20 and49–50 says the prior Crab marker is discarded. An audit that cancels Coup before assessing Crab movement avoids relying on the unresolved Coup-over-Crab movement interpretation. It must also let Under Elf Hill's one-turn movement restriction expire before expecting a legal Crab move.

Confidence boundary: the marker-persistence contract is explicit locally; the full Crab→Confabulation→Coup→Under Elf Hill combination is not an express published example in the pages reviewed. The combination conclusion follows from those local rules plus the published component-marker rule. This review does not determine any engine behavior or whether a particular fixture correctly reaches the state described.
