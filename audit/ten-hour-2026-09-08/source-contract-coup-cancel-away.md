# Coup cancellation while its replacement King is away

Conclusion: cancellation is supported by the general local contract, provided restoring the original Prince to King is legal. I found no requirement that both physical pieces affected by Coup must currently be on the board. The exact combination is not answered explicitly in the inspected publisher rulings, so this is a rule application, not a quoted card-specific ruling or an engine bug claim.

## Explicit source rules

- `rules.md` §10 retains Continuing Effect cards even when their initial conditions cease; suspension does not make them disappear, and Peace Talks can remove them. The section associates markers with physical identity.
- `rules.md` §15.3 makes the original King a capturable Prince and its replacement royal. Its explicit Peace Talks restriction concerns loss of the Prince when cancellation would leave no King. It does not prohibit cancellation merely because the replacement is temporarily absent.
- `rules.md` §22.6 and `cards.md`, Peace Talks, permit cancellation of one Continuing Effect. The Checkmate Rule still bars illegal King loss or an equivalent immediate victory. A safe surviving original Prince does not satisfy that stated prohibition.
- `rules.md` §22.4 treats Under Elf Hill as temporary absence, not capture or death. It preserves the departing piece's identity and attached effects, explicitly leaves a Prince on the board, and explicitly says Peace Talks cannot cancel Under Elf Hill itself because it is not a Continuing Effect.
- Section 22.4 attaches the eventual movement restriction to the physical returning piece; a subsequent change in royal status does not transfer that restriction to another piece.
- `rules.md` §17 concerns cancellation/rollback of cards or moves. It supplies no additional requirement that both Coup pieces must be on board before a retained Coup can be canceled. Peace Talks cancellation of an ongoing transformation should not be confused with replaying the original whole turn backward.

Publisher checks used the preserved `official-faq.pdf`, printed pages 19–21, 50, and 62. Pages 19 and 50 describe cancellation of a Confabulation that also removes its dependent Coup: the Prince becomes King again, with immunity from Peace Talks if the Prince has been captured. Pages 20–21 describe suspension of Coup and other transformations; they add no general on-board-both-pieces condition. Page 62 addresses Under Elf Hill's mandatory return and distinguishes a turn with no move from stalemate when no return square exists; it does not address canceling Coup during absence. `cards.md` contains no additional restriction in the three card bodies inspected: Coup, Peace Talks, and Under Elf Hill.

## Consequences of applying those rules

If legal Peace Talks removes Coup while its replacement is away, the surviving original Prince should regain royal status on its existing square. The away replacement should lose the royalty supplied by that canceled Coup and retain its own physical identity and ordinary role. These are the ordinary consequences of removing the transformation, applied to an off-board physical piece.

The separate Under Elf Hill obligation should remain attached to the same departing physical piece. Cancellation of Coup does not cancel Under Elf Hill, make that piece captured, or transfer the pending return to the restored King already on board. The same piece should return at the scheduled time, and its one-turn movement restriction should remain on it. This follows from the local identity and non-transfer provisions; no inspected FAQ example states this exact sequence.

The inspected texts do not expressly resolve every placement detail once the returning piece has ceased to be royal. In particular, whether the former King's destination must still satisfy Under Elf Hill's literal no-check/safe-destination condition requires interpreting that condition after loss of royalty. Do not use this review as a ruling on that narrower placement question. It does support investigating a blanket refusal to cancel the retained Coup solely because its replacement King is away, using a fixture where restoration and eventual return are safe under either interpretation.
