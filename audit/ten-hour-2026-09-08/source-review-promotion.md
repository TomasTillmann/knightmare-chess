# Ordinary promotion and Masquerade: source review

Conclusion: the reviewed sources distinguish ordinary promotion from a continuing-effect transformation, but do not conclusively state whether a normally promoted Pawn retains Pawn identity for Masquerade eligibility. Treating it as eligible is a reasonable reading, not an explicit ruling.

- Official rulebook, PDF page 1, “Transformed Pieces”: the original-piece targeting rule is introduced specifically for pieces transformed by continuing-effect cards.
- Official rulebook, PDF page 2, “Terminology”: a transformed piece has a new name and powers from a continuing-effect card. Ordinary promotion does not satisfy that definition. “Special Pawn Rules” on the same page permits promotion to Queen, Rook, Knight, or Bishop; it does not discuss retained Pawn targeting identity.
- Official FAQ, printed/PDF page 7: the promoted-Pawn answer concerns physical replacement and resurrection accounting. It says the Pawn can remain marked as its new type, or be treated as dead if a captured piece is used as its marker, while that captured piece remains available. This establishes piece accounting, but does not expressly apply Pawn-targeted or Pawn-excluding cards to a promoted piece.
- Official FAQ, printed/PDF page 8: Crabs and Princes retain original-piece targeting through the transformed-pieces rule. This supports the narrower continuing-effect reading. It does not resolve ordinary promotion.
- Local `cards.md`, lines 121–123: Masquerade excludes a Pawn but does not address promotion.

Uncertainty: the FAQ phrase describing the Pawn as still on the board could be read as retaining identity, but its surrounding question is about resurrecting physical pieces. Applying it to Masquerade would require an interpretation beyond the express answer. No engine or test sources were inspected.
