# Composite targeting source review

Sources: repository `cards.md` (Bog, lines 53–55; Toll, lines 89–91; Confabulation, lines 109–111), and Steve Jackson Games, *Knightmare Chess: Official Rulings*, 28 February 2003, printed/PDF pages 12–13 and 18–19 (`official-faq.pdf`). This review makes no claims about engine behavior.

## Rook–Knight composite and Bog

**Conclusion: yes**, when the composite makes a Rook move of at least two squares, subject to the ordinary legality restrictions on Bog. Which physical component represents the composite cannot change eligibility.

**Explicit rules:** Confabulation makes the two pieces one new piece that can move, capture, and be affected by cards like either component. Bog names Rooks, Bishops, and Queens moving at least two squares. FAQ p. 12 distinguishes identity from temporary movement: a Knight merely moving as a Queen remains immune, while a Bishop moving as a Queen can be Bogged. It also says a Queen–Knight composite making a Knight move cannot be Bogged (repeated on p. 18). FAQ p. 13 clarifies shortened movement with obstacles and multiple moves; it supplies no exception based on a composite's representation.

**Reasoned implication:** the Rook component supplies eligible identity, and the actual multi-square Rook move supplies eligible movement. A Knight move would fail the movement requirement. The card says to move the two components together; no reviewed rule selects a privileged physical component for card targeting.

## Pawn–Knight composite as Toll payment

**Conclusion: yes**: its owner can choose the composite as the Pawn payment and lose the whole composite.

**Explicit rules:** Toll requires the mover to choose a Pawn to lose. Confabulation allows the new piece to be affected by cards like either component. FAQ pp. 18–19 treats a two-Pawn composite as only one piece for Annexation and Onslaught. FAQ p. 19 also bases Coup eligibility on whether the composite includes a Rook or Queen, confirming that component identities matter to card applicability.

**Reasoned implication:** the Pawn component makes the Pawn–Knight composite an eligible Toll selection; the selected object is the single merged piece, so payment removes that composite, rather than peeling off only its Pawn component. The reviewed FAQ pages do not explicitly answer this exact Toll combination.

## Contradictions and limits

No actual contradictory wording was found in the reviewed sources. The FAQ's prohibition on Bogging a Knight merely moving as a Queen concerns temporary movement, whereas Confabulation explicitly grants card applicability through either component. The explicit immunity of a composite making a Knight move is likewise compatible with Bogging its qualifying Rook move. FAQ p. 18's exception for a continuing effect that creates a distinct named piece (such as Crab) concerns how that continuing effect attaches to the composite; it does not state a general exception to the Confabulation targeting rule or authorize splitting a Toll payment.
