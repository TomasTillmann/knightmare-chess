# Chaos after an optional after-move card

**Publisher answer: yes.** Assuming the mover legally completes Ng1–f3 and then plays Crab or Curse as that turn's optional after-move card, the opponent may respond with Chaos, Knightmare!, or Think Again! once that turn's actions are complete. The publisher FAQ expressly says: “They are played after his entire turn, including his optional card play.” This directly resolves the timing question; the optional card is part of the cancellation opportunity, rather than an intervening event that destroys it. Reference: `official-faq.pdf`, printed/PDF page 16, the “What ‘move’ … cancel?” question; repeated on page 37. This establishes timing, without deciding every rollback detail for Crab or Curse.

**Local contract differs if that card play is classified as unrelated.** `rules.md` §17.1, line 512, explicitly places Chaos after the completed move but before turn end, says a passive card is not a new cancellation trigger, and closes the immediate window for an unrelated intervening action. It does not explicitly name Crab or Curse or define their optional play as unrelated; nevertheless, interpreting them that way yields rejection under the local contract and conflicts with the publisher's express timing. The passive-card sentence alone says no new trigger, which does not logically establish that an existing trigger must close. Lines 518 and 520 apply Chaos's contract to both aliases.

This window-closing language is **an explicit local contract**, not text marked “Recommended ruling.” The separately labeled recommendation at line 510 concerns whether the replacement move differs. Do not present the local window rule as publisher-authoritative or silently replace it during an engine-only correction.

The printed effects in `cards.md` (Chaos lines 289–291; Knightmare! lines 305–307; Think Again! lines 317–319) cancel the move, require a different move, and permit taking back a played card. They do not settle the timing themselves. FAQ pages 17/38 additionally require a different board displacement even if an unrelated card is omitted on replay; page 37 distinguishes move cancellation with optional card retrieval from Fog of War's cancellation of a card itself.

**Disposition:** record a source-contract discrepancy, not an engine defect established solely by the publisher FAQ. Publisher fidelity requires preserving the response opportunity through the mover's own optional after-move card; the repository must clarify what counts as an unrelated intervening action before that interpretation can be treated as its explicit acceptance contract. No engine or test source was inspected.
