# Revenge: captor-check source contract

**Eligibility and roles.** Revenge's artwork OCR says, “Play when your Pawn is captured without a card” (`artwork-ocr.json`, entry `id: revenge`, image `/KC6_card2.png`). Its effect removes one opposing Pawn and regards it as captured (`cards.md:85–87`; same artwork). The reactor is the player whose Pawn was captured; the active mover is the opponent who made that capture and has completed the Regular Move. Removing a Pawn shielding that mover's King gives check to the mover, not to the reactor.

**Explicit general rules.** An opponent may respond during the acting player's turn (§8.3, `rules.md:170–172`); a card grants no extra move unless it says so (§8.2, `rules.md:168`). After all effects resolve, the acting King's safety must be verified (§8.4, `rules.md:178–182`). Regular-card check is generally allowed (§11.2, `rules.md:242–246`), but §11.6 expressly says that after the Regular Move, a regular card leaving the acting King checked has no board effect: restore the pre-card position while spending, discarding, and replacing the card (`rules.md:267–275`). Revenge supplies no exception or extra move; no Revenge-specific subsection was found in the local rules.

**Conclusion and ambiguity.** A permanently live position with the active mover checked and no legal move, card, or end-turn action is not defensible under these sources. Read “acting King” as the King of the turn's active mover (§8), and §11.6 directly supplies the correction: Revenge's capture fizzles, the Pawn returns, and Revenge is spent. The mechanism is explicit in the general rule; applying it to an opponent's reaction requires the role interpretation, which Revenge's artwork does not independently settle. If “acting” instead means the player currently resolving the card, §11.6 does not directly prescribe this mover-check remedy; the need for a valid resolution remains supported by §8.4, while the exact remedy would require a ruling. Neither reading authorizes an indefinite live deadlock, an extra move, or an unprovided turn-boundary exception. Nonmating check must not be silently classified as card-created board mate (§11.1–11.2).

**Hostage comparison.** This matches the same observed post-reaction deadlock family described in `source-contract-hostage-check.md`: an opponent's reaction exposes the already-moved active King's check while its Regular Move remains consumed. Hostage has additional card-specific text; Revenge does not. The common source-level safety question does not establish a shared implementation root cause.
